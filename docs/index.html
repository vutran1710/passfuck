passfuck

Remember one phrase. Get every password on your Mac and iPhone.

curl -fsSL https://vutran1710.github.io/passfuck/install | bash

Paste it into Terminal on your Mac. A guided setup takes about ten minutes. Read the script before you run it.

Follow the illustrated tutorial to see every step on Mac and iPhone.

How it works

  1. You invent one phraseIt's the only thing you ever need to remember.
  2. It becomes your keyThe same phrase makes the same key on any machine, so there's no key file to lose.
  3. Passwords live in your repoEach one is encrypted and stored on your own GitHub, which any device can download.
  4. Your devices fill them inMac and iPhone read from the repo, and every change syncs back to it.

Where you use it

In the browser
Click the Browserpass button in Brave or Chrome, type the alias, and it fills the login.
In any Mac app
Open Raycast, type the alias, and copy or paste the password. This works even in apps that hide their login fields.
On iPhone
Tap Passwords above the keyboard, choose Pass, and search for the alias. Or open the Pass app and tap to copy.
In Terminal
pass -c github.com copies a password. pass generate newsite.com 24 makes a new one.

Every entry is saved under an alias you choose, like gh or bank-main, rather than the website's name. That keeps your list of sites private, but it means autofill can't guess the entry from the page you're on. You type the alias to find it.

On your iPhone

There's no app to build or download from us. Your iPhone uses Pass - Password Store, a free, open-source app on the App Store that has been around since 2017. It reads your vault directly, and we checked that it opens keys made from a phrase. Setup takes about five minutes, and the tutorial shows each screen.

  1. Install Pass - Password Store from the App Store.
  2. On your Mac, run pf phone. A folder opens with your key, an access key for your vault, and instructions.
  3. AirDrop the folder to your iPhone and save it to Files.
  4. In the Pass app's settings, import the two keys from Files and enter your vault's address from the instructions. When it asks for your phrase, let it remember it so Face ID can unlock it from then on.
  5. In iPhone Settings, open General, then AutoFill & Passwords, and turn on Pass.
  6. Delete the folder from your iPhone and your Mac.

New passwords you save in the app sync back to your vault, and your Mac sees them within ten minutes.

What's public and what isn't

Anyone can see

  • The aliases you give your entries, and how many there are
  • When each entry was added or changed
  • The name and email used to make your key

Only you can read

  • Which website each entry is for
  • Passwords
  • Usernames and emails
  • Notes and one-time codes

Pick aliases that mean something only to you. Renaming one later won't erase the old name, because the vault keeps its full history.

Because the repo is public, anyone can download it and try to guess your phrase on their own computers. Each guess costs them about ten seconds and a gigabyte of memory. That stops casual attempts, but a long-running attacker could still try billions, so the phrase itself has to be strong.

Choosing your phrase

  • Use at least six or seven words.
  • Make it a sentence only you would come up with. Never use a lyric, quote or saying.
  • Leave out pet names, birthdays and addresses.
  • Type it exactly the same way every time, including capitals and spaces.
  • Write it on paper and keep it somewhere safe. If you lose it, nobody can recover your passwords.
